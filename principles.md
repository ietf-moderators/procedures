# Principles for moderation decisions

**Purpose.** The procedures exist to stop disruption, discourage it, and protect other participants' ability to take part in forums, rather than to punish. A remedy longer than 28 days is justified only by a continuing risk of disruption shown by the person's own conduct, including a run of recent incidents.

## Deciding whether there is an incident

**Conduct, not position.** Conduct is judged by what was done, not by the position taken or who took it.

**Reasonable reader.** Conduct is judged as a reasonable participant would read it, allowing for differences in language, culture and experience of the IETF, until the person has had a clear warning about the same conduct.

**Evidence.** An incident is recorded only when it is more likely than not, based upon the material the administrator has seen, that the conduct was disruptive. The more serious the likely consequences of the finding, including for later remedies, the more secure it must be. A pattern across messages can establish disruption.

**Warning first.** For judgement-based triggers ([off-topic messages](procedures.md#handling-messages-unrelated-to-the-forums-purpose), [hindering the group's work](procedures.md#handling-behavior-that-hinders-the-work-of-the-group), [sealioning](procedures.md#what-counts-as-disruption), [slop](procedures.md#handling-slop), discourtesy short of attacks on people, and [failing to disclose a relevant interest](procedures.md#handling-astroturfing)), a conversation or reminder comes before a recorded incident, unless the person has already been asked to stop the same or similar conduct.

## Choosing a remedy

**Proportionality.** Remedies must be proportional to the seriousness of the conduct (its effect on people and on the group's work, and whether it was deliberate) and to how likely it is to recur. The maximum is a ceiling, not a starting point.

**Aggravating and mitigating factors.** Continuing after a clear warning to stop is an aggravating factor in applying a remedy, as is attempting to evade a hold or ban. Merely asking for a review or appeal is never an aggravating factor. Mitigating factors include prompt acknowledgement or correction, a proportionate response to serious provocation, and not knowing a forum's process norms because of being new to the IETF.

**Earlier incidents.** Decide how serious the conduct is before considering earlier incidents. The action-taker sees only the maximum remedy that the register allows, not the earlier incidents themselves. When the moderator team or the IESG reviews a decision, they weigh earlier incidents as a whole, not as a count: earlier incidents count for more when they are serious, recent or part of a pattern with the present conduct, and for less when they are old or isolated.

**Combined effect.** Recording the incident (which raises the maximum for later remedies), any remedy, any public statement that names the person and any extension to other forums must together be proportionate.

## Fairness and oversight

**Reasons.** The private notice to the respondent must say why this remedy and length were chosen (a short statement is enough) and how to ask for review or appeal.

**Impartiality.** Anyone deciding or reviewing a matter must step aside if they are a party, the target of personal attacks in the conduct under consideration, or otherwise personally involved; the decision-maker or the moderator team decides whether this applies. A co-administrator acts instead or, failing that, the moderator team without the person who stepped aside.

**Consistency.** Decisions about what counts as disruption, and about the remedy for it, should be broadly consistent across similar cases. Decisions may differ because the facts differ, but not so much that a reasonable and disinterested observer would see favour or disfavour towards particular individuals, or towards participants because of their role, language, or length of time in the IETF.
